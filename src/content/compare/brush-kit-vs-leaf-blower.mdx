## Side-by-side

| | Drill brush kit | Leaf blower |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How it works | Flexible rods + brush spin through 4" duct; lint is loosened and pulled/pushed in controlled sections | High-volume air pushed through the duct to dislodge and eject lint |
| Best setup | Dryer disconnected; brush from dryer toward outdoors (or staged sections); vacuum at ends | Dryer disconnected; blower at one end with a clear exit at the outdoor cap |
| Pros | Mechanical contact with stuck lint mats; works in moderate bends; common DIY kit type | Fast on short, mostly clear runs; no brush rods to assemble |
| Cons | Time and patience; wrong brush size can jam; easy to push a plug deeper if you don’t vacuum | Messy outdoor blowout; can force lint into cavities if the duct is damaged; loud; poor on packed elbows |
| Mess level | Contained if you vacuum as you go | High — lint cloud outdoors (and indoors if sealed poorly) |
| Skill / risk | Moderate — don’t force rods; watch gas lines when moving dryer | Moderate–high — eye/lung protection; never blow into a connected dryer; watch for disconnected joints |

## When to pick each

**Pick a drill brush kit when:**

- The run is accessible and you can disconnect at the dryer.
- Lint feels packed (weak airflow after clearing the outdoor cap).
- You have a vacuum to collect what the brush loosens.
- You want a method that scrapes the duct wall, not only moves air.

**Pick a leaf blower only when (narrow cases):**

- The duct is short, mostly straight, already known metal and intact.
- The outdoor termination is open and you can stand clear of the blowout.
- You are finishing after brushing, to clear loose dust — not as the only tool on a clogged vent.

For most homeowners deciding DIY vs pro, start with the [DIY vs pro guide](/ventruncheck/guides/diy-vs-pro-dryer-vent-cleaning). If the run is long, roof-terminated, or unknown, skip both DIY methods and hire full-run cleaning.

## Where each approach fails

**Brush kit fails when:**

- Rods are too short for the run and you pretend the near section is “done.”
- You push a dense plug past a bend into an unreachable section.
- Foil duct collapses or tears under the brush.
- You cannot safely access a roof cap to finish the job.

**Leaf blower fails when:**

- The vent is heavily packed — air channels through a small hole and leaves mats in place.
- Joints are loose; pressure blows duct sections apart inside walls or the chase.
- You blow toward the dryer or with the dryer still connected — lint and debris can enter the appliance.
- Screened or damaged caps redirect lint into the wall cavity.
- Multi-story or shared chases — you cannot see where the air (and lint) goes.

**Shared stop rules:** burning smell, smoke, or scorched lint → **stop using the dryer** and get qualified help. Do not keep blowing or brushing “until it works.” After either method, run the [before-you-run checklist](/ventruncheck/checklist/before-you-run-the-dryer-again).

## Side-by-side

| | Periscope (flat) dryer vent | Flexible metal transition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How it works | Rigid flat metal box with offset openings bridges a shallow gap between dryer outlet and wall/floor fitting | Semi-rigid or flexible aluminum metal hose (not foil/vinyl) bends to connect the same two fittings |
| Best setup | Dryer sits close to the wall (often under ~6 inches of clearance) and outlets nearly line up left/right or up/down | A little more depth behind the dryer; you can keep a short, gentle curve without crushing |
| Pros | Thin profile; holds shape when the dryer is pushed back; less “accordion crush” than cheap foil | Adapts to small misalignment; widely available; easy to length-trim for a short run |
| Cons | Wrong size/offset leaves gaps or forced elbows; joints must clamp tight; not a fix for a clogged wall chase | Too long or too many bends recreate kinks; still foil/vinyl impostors on store shelves |
| Mess / maintenance | Smooth-ish interior if metal and sealed; still inspect clamps and the outdoor cap seasonally | Ridges can hold lint if over-length; pull and check after cleaning |
| Skill / risk | Moderate — measure offset, support the dryer, watch gas lines/electric | Moderate — choose real metal, keep it short, clamp (no screws into the airstream) |

## When to pick each

**Prefer a metal periscope when:**

- The dryer barely clears the wall and a round flex hose would crush flat when you push the unit back.
- Outlet and wall fitting are offset a few inches (side or vertical) and a flat duct is designed for that offset.
- You already replaced foil/vinyl once and the crush returned because depth is the real constraint — see also [metal vs foil transition](/ventruncheck/compare/metal-transition-vs-foil-duct).
- Slow drying persists after a clear outdoor hood and lint screen, and the bottleneck is the smashed behind-dryer section ([slow-drying checklist](/ventruncheck/checklist/slow-drying-clothes-checklist)).

**Prefer a short flexible metal transition when:**

- You have enough depth for a gentle arc without pressing the hose against the wall.
- Outlets nearly align and you only need a few inches of metal — not a coiled “extra” length.
- You are reassembling after a clean and want a standard semi-rigid piece you can pull for the next [behind-the-dryer inspection](/ventruncheck/checklist/behind-the-dryer-inspection).
- A periscope’s fixed offset does not match your dryer/outlet geometry without awkward adapters.

## Where each approach fails

**Periscope fails when:**

- The model’s offset does not match the dryer and wall fittings, so you force a leaky joint or extra elbows.
- Only the last two feet look “metal” while the wall or roof chase is still packed — symptoms return ([when the run is too long](/ventruncheck/guides/when-dryer-vent-run-is-too-long)).
- Plastic or thin stamped parts warp near hot exhaust; treat melting or scorch like a stop condition.
- You omit clamps or leave sharp screws in the airstream that seed a new lint plug.

**Flexible metal transition fails when:**

- You buy foil or vinyl labeled as “dryer duct” and crush it on install.
- Extra length coils behind the dryer and acts like a clog even after a cap wipe.
- The dryer is shoved back hard enough to flatten the hose into a slit.
- You treat the transition swap as a full-run clean when the outdoor flap still sticks or the chase is unknown.

**Shared stop rules:** burning smell, smoke, or scorched lint → **stop using the dryer** and get qualified help ([burning smell guide](/ventruncheck/guides/burning-smell-from-dryer)). Long runs, roof terminations, multi-story chases, or unknown paths are pro territory for a true dryer-to-outdoors clean — not a prettier transition only. After any swap or clean, run the [before-you-run checklist](/ventruncheck/checklist/before-you-run-the-dryer-again).

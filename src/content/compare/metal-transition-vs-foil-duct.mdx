## Side-by-side

| | Rigid / semi-rigid metal | Foil or vinyl flexible |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How it works | Smooth or lightly corrugated metal pipe connects dryer outlet to the wall/floor fitting; clamps hold joints | Thin accordion foil or plastic hose snakes into the gap behind the dryer |
| Best setup | Dryer pulled out enough to seat short, nearly straight metal; no sharp kinks | Only as a temporary stopgap — not a lasting install |
| Pros | Better airflow; less lint catch; holds shape when the dryer is pushed back; widely recommended for the short transition | Cheap and easy to “make fit” in a tight space |
| Cons | Needs a few inches of clearance and correct length/elbows; slightly more install care | Crushes flat, tears, traps lint in ridges; vinyl can melt near hot exhaust |
| Mess / maintenance | Lower ongoing lint in the short section if joints are sealed | Ridges pack lint fast; crushed sections act like a clog |
| Skill / risk | Moderate — move dryer carefully (watch gas lines); use clamps, avoid screws into the airstream | Low install effort, higher long-term heat/fire and airflow risk |

## When to pick each

**Pick rigid or semi-rigid metal when:**

- You can pull the dryer and see crushed foil, vinyl, or a kinked accordion hose behind it.
- Clothes take extra cycles and the outdoor cap looks mostly clear — the bottleneck is often the transition.
- You want the short behind-dryer section to survive being pushed back against the wall.
- A pro or DIY full-run clean is done and you are reassembling for lasting airflow.

**“Pick” foil or vinyl only when (narrow / temporary):**

- You are documenting what is already installed so you can replace it — not choosing it as the goal.
- An emergency reconnect after a move, with a firm plan to swap to metal before regular use resumes.
- Never as the permanent answer for a gas or electric dryer that runs hot loads (towels, bedding).

## Where each approach fails

**Metal transition fails when:**

- You use too many elbows or an oversized length that coils and recreates a kink.
- Joints are loose and dump lint under the dryer.
- Screws protrude into the duct and snag lint into a new plug.
- You leave a long hidden run unrepaired and only swap the last two feet — symptoms return.

**Foil / vinyl flexible fails when:**

- The dryer is shoved back and the hose flattens into a slit.
- Ridges fill with lint until airflow collapses even after a “cap clean.”
- Vinyl softens or melts near hot exhaust — **stop the dryer** if you smell burning or see scorched material.
- Wildlife or vacuum tools tear thin foil mid-run and leak lint into the cavity.

**Shared stop rules:** burning smell, smoke, or scorched lint → **stop using the dryer** and get qualified help. Long runs, roof terminations, or unknown chases still need full-run attention (often a pro), not only a prettier transition.

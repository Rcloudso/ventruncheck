## Side-by-side

| | Exterior wall termination | Roof termination |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How it works | Short or moderate duct runs through a side/rear wall to an outdoor hood or cap with a flap or screen | Duct climbs vertically through floors or attic then exits a roof cap; longer equivalent length and more elbows are common |
| Best setup | Dryer on an exterior wall or with a clear, mostly horizontal metal run to that wall | House layout forces an upward chase (interior laundry, multi-story) **and** the duct is metal, sealed, and serviceable |
| Pros | Usually shorter path; outdoor hood is easy to see and clear; DIY seasonal checks are realistic | Can be the only code-compliant path for interior rooms; keeps exhaust off walkways if siting is constrained |
| Cons | Bad siting dumps moist air onto decks, AC pads, or neighboring windows; crushed transitions still choke short runs | Harder to inspect; attic/roof work; longer runs pack lint mid-chase; DIY full-run cleans often miss the middle |
| Mess / maintenance | Cap lint and nests are visible from ground level; quick flap check after storms | Roof/attic access, fall risk, and weather; partial end cleans leave plugs in the chase |
| Skill / risk | Moderate DIY on short accessible metal; still stop for burning smell | Higher — roof/attic, unknown chases, and long equivalent length usually need a pro full-run clean |

## When to pick each

**Prefer (or keep) an exterior wall termination when:**

- The dryer already sits on or near an exterior wall and a short, nearly straight metal path is possible.
- You want a hood you can inspect without ladders — stuck flaps, nests, and lint mats show up fast.
- Dry times crept up and a ground-level [outdoor cap checklist](/ventruncheck/checklist/outdoor-vent-cap-inspection) plus behind-dryer look is enough to start troubleshooting.
- You are replacing foil/vinyl with metal and can keep the run short — see [metal vs foil transition](/ventruncheck/compare/metal-transition-vs-foil-duct).

**Roof termination is appropriate only when (narrow / layout-driven):**

- Interior laundry or stacked floors leave no safe exterior wall path that meets dryer and local length/elbow limits.
- The existing chase is already roof-terminated, intact metal, and you are planning **full-run** service — not “roof cap wipe only.”
- A qualified installer sized equivalent length, used smooth metal where required, and left cleaning access; you are not adding a booster to mask a packed attic run ([booster vs clean](/ventruncheck/compare/booster-fan-vs-cleaning-clog)).
- You accept that seasonal maintenance means pro or skilled attic/roof work, not a five-minute DIY.

## Where each approach fails

**Wall termination fails when:**

- The outdoor hood faces a tight soffit, fence, or AC intake and moist linty air recirculates or ices the flap shut.
- Screens or decorative covers choke airflow; birds nest in uncapped openings.
- Only the last two feet are metal while the wall chase still holds crushed flex or a mid-wall plug.
- You clear the hood and lint screen but never check the transition — symptoms return ([lint screen vs full clean](/ventruncheck/compare/lint-screen-vs-full-vent-cleaning)).

**Roof termination fails when:**

- Someone cleans only the roof cap and calls the attic chase “done.”
- The vertical run uses foil, vinyl, or unsealed joints that dump lint into the cavity.
- Equivalent length (pipe + elbows) exceeds the dryer maker’s limit, so heat and humidity linger even with a “clean” look at the ends.
- DIY attic work leaves gaps, screws in the airstream, or no way to re-service the run later.

**Shared stop rules:** burning smell, smoke, or scorched lint → **stop using the dryer** and get qualified help ([burning smell guide](/ventruncheck/guides/burning-smell-from-dryer)). Long, roof-terminated, multi-story, or unknown chases are pro territory for a true dryer-to-outdoors clean — not a leaf blower from one end. After any clean or termination work, run the [before-you-run checklist](/ventruncheck/checklist/before-you-run-the-dryer-again).

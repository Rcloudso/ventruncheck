## Side-by-side

| | Lint screen every load | Full dryer-to-outdoors vent clean |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How it works | Pull the filter before/after each load; remove the lint mat so room air can enter the dryer | Disconnect or access the duct; brush/vacuum or hire a pro so the **entire** path from dryer outlet to outdoor termination is clear |
| Best setup | Every household, every load — non-negotiable baseline | After symptoms (slow dry, hot room, weak flap), on a planned interval, or when the run is long/unknown |
| Pros | Fast, free, prevents a common airflow choke at the machine; required by every dryer maker | Removes lint packs mid-run and at the cap that the screen never reaches; restores exhaust and lowers heat risk from trapped lint |
| Cons | Alone does **not** clear the hose, wall chase, or outdoor hood | Takes time or a service fee; DIY on long/roof runs is incomplete and risky |
| Mess / maintenance | Small lint ball in the trash; wipe the slot occasionally | Contained if you vacuum as you brush; outdoor blowout if someone only uses a blower |
| Skill / risk | Very low | Moderate DIY on short accessible metal runs; pro for long, roof, multi-story, or unknown ducts |

## When to pick each

**Always do the lint screen (not optional):**

- Before or after every load — a packed screen alone can double dry time and overheat the cabinet.
- If the screen tears or warps, replace the filter type your dryer uses; do not run without one.
- Wipe lint from the recess under the screen so the seal seats flat.

**Add a full dryer-to-outdoors vent clean when:**

- Clothes need two or three cycles, the laundry room runs hot, or the outdoor flap barely moves — see [signs of a clog](/ventruncheck/guides/signs-clogged-dryer-vent).
- You clear the screen religiously but lint still mats the outdoor cap or the floor behind the dryer.
- You find crushed foil/vinyl behind the dryer — fix the transition (see [metal vs foil](/ventruncheck/compare/metal-transition-vs-foil-duct)) **and** clear the rest of the run.
- The run is long (~25+ ft equivalent), has many elbows, or ends on a roof — prefer a **pro full-run clean** with written scope to the termination; partial DIY of the first few feet is a common trap ([DIY vs pro](/ventruncheck/guides/diy-vs-pro-dryer-vent-cleaning)).

**Do not treat “I emptied the lint trap” as proof the vent is clear.** The screen and the duct are different choke points.

## Where each approach fails

**Lint-screen-only fails when:**

- You assume a clean filter means the hose and wall duct are fine — mid-run plugs still cook lint with heat.
- Foil or vinyl behind the dryer is crushed shut while the screen looks perfect.
- Birds or lint mats block the outdoor cap; indoor airflow “feels” fine at the filter.
- Someone sells a “dryer refresh” that only vacs the screen and calls the job done.

**Full-vent cleaning fails when:**

- The tech or DIYer only cleans the first 6–12 ft and leaves the plug in the middle.
- Joints stay loose and dump lint into the cavity after reconnect.
- Foil/vinyl stays in place and collapses again within weeks.
- You skip the outdoor termination check — see [outdoor vent cap checklist](/ventruncheck/checklist/outdoor-vent-cap-inspection) and [behind-the-dryer inspection](/ventruncheck/checklist/behind-the-dryer-inspection).

**Shared stop rules:** burning smell, smoke, or scorched lint → **stop using the dryer**, unplug or kill the breaker, and get qualified help before any “routine” clean. Do not run another load to confirm the smell. Long or roof-terminated runs you cannot safely reach belong with a pro, not deeper DIY. After any clean or reconnect, run the [before-you-run checklist](/ventruncheck/checklist/before-you-run-the-dryer-again). For interval planning after a full clean, see [how often to clean](/ventruncheck/guides/how-often-clean-dryer-vent).

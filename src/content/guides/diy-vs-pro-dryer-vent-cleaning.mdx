## The problem

Clothes that take two or three cycles to dry are often a vent problem, not a “weak dryer.” Lint buildup restricts airflow, raises dryer temperature, and increases fire risk. The risk goes up with long duct runs, multiple bends, and roof or wall caps that are clogged or crushed.

A common quote trap: a tech quotes a cheap “vent clean” but only clears the first **6–12 feet** from the dryer, leaving the rest of a long run packed. You get a temporary improvement, then the same symptoms return. Ask what length they clean, whether they disconnect at the dryer **and** open the outdoor termination, and whether they use a camera or airflow reading after the work.

**Safety first:** If you smell burning, see scorched lint, or the dryer cabinet is unusually hot, **stop using the dryer** and unplug it (or switch off the breaker). Do not keep running “just one more load.” This is not medical or legal advice — treat burning smells as a stop condition and get a qualified pro or electrician involved if needed.

## Decision tree

Work top to bottom. The first “call a pro / stop DIY” answer wins.

1. **Burning smell, smoke, scorched lint, or hot spots on the dryer?** → **Stop using the dryer.** Unplug / kill power. Do not DIY past this point until a qualified person inspects.
2. **Vent run longer than ~25 ft equivalent, or 3+ hard bends, or roof termination you cannot safely reach?** → Prefer a **pro** with full-run cleaning (dryer to termination), not a partial brush from the laundry room only.
3. **Flexible foil/vinyl accordion duct crushed behind the dryer?** → Replace with rigid or semi-rigid metal as part of the job (DIY if you can move the dryer safely; otherwise pro).
4. **Outdoor cap blocked by lint, nest, or stuck flap — and you can reach it safely from ground level?** → Clear the cap first; many “dryer problems” are termination-only.
5. **Short, straight, accessible run (typical first-floor exterior wall), you can pull the dryer, and you own a brush kit?** → **DIY** is reasonable: disconnect, brush toward outdoors, vacuum lint, reassemble without crushing the duct.
6. **Rental, HOA restrictions, multi-unit chase, or you are unsure the duct is metal and continuous?** → **Pro** — avoid forcing tools into unknown shared or inaccessible runs.

## Quick comparison

| Factor | DIY | Pro |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Best for | Short, accessible wall runs | Long runs, roof caps, many elbows, unknowns |
| Typical tools | Drill brush kit, vacuum, screwdriver, flashlight | Rotary brushes, negative air / vac, camera, ladders |
| Cost pattern | Tool cost once; your time | Per-visit fee; ask what’s included |
| Quote trap | Overconfidence on long/hidden runs | Cleaning only the first 6–12 ft |
| Fail mode | Pushing lint deeper; damaging duct; ladder falls | Incomplete clean sold as “full service” |
| Stop rule | Burning smell → stop dryer, get help | Same — plus refuse partial cleans on long runs |

## Recommended types (not brands)

Prefer **types** of gear and service scopes over brand hype:

- **Dryer vent drill brush kit** (nylon brushes sized for 4" duct, flexible rods) — for short accessible runs. Example placeholder: [drill brush kit](/ventruncheck/resources?ref=PENDING#brush-kits).
- **Shop vacuum with hose adapters** — to pull lint at the dryer outlet and outdoor cap, not as a substitute for brushing a packed run.

## Checklist

Before you choose DIY or book a pro:

- [ ] Confirm no burning smell, smoke, or scorched lint — if yes, **stop the dryer**.
- [ ] Measure or estimate run length and count elbows; note roof vs wall termination.
- [ ] Look behind the dryer: crushed foil? kinks? plastic duct?
- [ ] Inspect outdoor flap/cap for lint mats or nests (only if safe to access).
- [ ] For DIY: can you pull the dryer, disconnect the duct, and reassemble without damaging gas lines or power cords?
- [ ] For pro quotes: ask if they clean **full length**, open the termination, and what happens if the duct is damaged.
- [ ] After any clean: use the [pre-use checklist](/ventruncheck/checklist/before-you-run-the-dryer-again) before the next load.

When in doubt on long or complex runs, hire a pro rather than forcing tools through an unknown chase.

## The problem

A clogged dryer vent rarely announces itself with a single dramatic failure. More often, drying time creeps up, the laundry room gets warmer, lint piles at the outdoor cap, or the dryer “works” but never quite finishes a load. People blame a weak machine, humidity, or “old clothes” — and keep running heat through a restricted duct.

This page is a **symptom → next action** guide. It is not a fire inspection, insurance checklist, or appliance diagnosis for every brand.

**Safety first:** If you smell burning, see scorched or brown lint, or the dryer cabinet feels unusually hot, **stop using the dryer** and unplug it (or switch off the breaker). Do not run “one more load” to confirm the smell. Long or roof-terminated runs you cannot safely reach belong with a qualified pro, not deeper DIY probing.

## Decision tree

Work top to bottom. The first matching row wins.

1. **Burning smell, smoke, scorched lint, glowing spots, or unusually hot dryer exterior?** → **Stop the dryer now.** Power off. Do not brush or blow into the duct until a qualified person inspects.
2. **Clothes need two or three cycles, or a normal load takes far longer than it used to?** → Treat as a **vent airflow problem until proven otherwise**. Check the outdoor flap motion and the short transition behind the dryer before replacing the appliance.
3. **Laundry room or dryer top gets much hotter mid-cycle than before?** → Restricted exhaust often dumps heat indoors. Clean / inspect the full run; do not ignore heat as “normal.”
4. **Outdoor flap barely moves, stays shut, or you see thick lint mats / nests at the cap?** → Clear or inspect the termination (ground-level only if safe). A clear-looking hood with **weak** air still means a mid-run clog.
5. **Lint screen is clean every load, but lint still blankets the floor behind the dryer or packs the outdoor hood?** → The duct or crushed foil transition is likely trapping lint — not only the screen.
6. **Symptoms after a move, remodel, or shoving the dryer tight to the wall?** → Look for a **kinked or crushed** transition hose first; replace foil/vinyl with metal when you can. See [metal vs foil](/ventruncheck/compare/metal-transition-vs-foil-duct).
7. **Long run (~25+ ft equivalent), many elbows, or roof cap — and DIY only reaches the ends?** → Prefer a **pro full-run clean** (dryer to termination). Partial cleans of the first 6–12 ft are a common trap — see [DIY vs pro](/ventruncheck/guides/diy-vs-pro-dryer-vent-cleaning).

## Symptom table

| Symptom | Likely meaning | Sensible next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Burning smell / scorched lint | Heat + lint risk | **Stop dryer**; qualified inspection before any “routine” clean |
| Two–three cycles to dry | Poor airflow | Cap check + transition check + full-run clean as needed |
| Hot laundry room / hot dryer top | Exhaust backing up | Same as above; do not keep running heavy towel loads |
| Outdoor flap weak or stuck | Cap clog, nest, or mid-run plug | Inspect/clear cap if safe; if still weak, full run |
| Clean lint screen, still lots of lint elsewhere | Duct or crushed hose trapping lint | Inspect behind dryer; plan brush/pro clean |
| New after pushing dryer back | Crushed flexible duct | Reseat or replace with rigid/semi-rigid metal |
| Fine after a cheap “vent clean,” then returns | Incomplete scope | Ask for full dryer-to-termination next time |

## Recommended types (not brands)

- **Outdoor cap inspection habit** — quick seasonal look at flap motion and lint mats (ground-level only). Related: [outdoor vent cap checklist](/ventruncheck/checklist/outdoor-vent-cap-inspection).
- **Dryer vent drill brush kit** — short, accessible wall runs between pro visits. Placeholder: [drill brush kit](/ventruncheck/resources?ref=PENDING#brush-kits).
- **Rigid or semi-rigid metal transition duct** — if the piece behind the dryer is crushed foil or vinyl. Placeholder: [metal transition duct](/ventruncheck/resources?ref=PENDING#transition-duct).
- **Full-run professional cleaning** — written scope to the outdoor termination, especially for long/roof runs. Placeholder: [pro full-run cleaning](/ventruncheck/resources?ref=PENDING#pro-service).
- **Interval planning** — after you clear the clog, set the next date with [how often to clean](/ventruncheck/guides/how-often-clean-dryer-vent).

Avoid treating a leaf blower as proof the whole run is clear on unknown ducts — see [brush kit vs leaf blower](/ventruncheck/compare/brush-kit-vs-leaf-blower).

## Checklist

- [ ] No burning smell, smoke, or scorched lint — if yes, **stop the dryer** first.
- [ ] Note drying time: one cycle vs two/three for a normal load.
- [ ] From outside (if safe): does the flap open with steady warm air?
- [ ] Behind the dryer: crushed foil, kinks, or loose joints?
- [ ] Lint screen clean — and still seeing heavy lint at the cap or on the floor?
- [ ] Estimate run length / elbows / wall vs roof termination.
- [ ] Choose DIY (short accessible) or pro (long/complex) using the [DIY vs pro guide](/ventruncheck/guides/diy-vs-pro-dryer-vent-cleaning).
- [ ] After any clean or reconnect, run the [before-you-run checklist](/ventruncheck/checklist/before-you-run-the-dryer-again).

Symptoms point to airflow and heat risk first — fix the vent path before assuming the dryer itself is “dead.”

<div class="callout warn">

**Red-flag stop conditions — do not keep running the dryer if any of these are true:**

- Burning smell, smoke, or scorched/brown lint behind the dryer or in the transition duct
- Dryer cabinet, rear panel, or exhaust air much hotter than usual
- Melted plastic, crushed foil, or vinyl hose still connecting the dryer to the wall
- Transition duct disconnected, leaking lint under the machine, or nearly flat
- Gas dryer: gas odor after you pull or push the machine (leave the area and follow your gas utility’s emergency guidance)
- Long hidden run with weak outdoor airflow after you only cleaned the first few feet — unfinished, not “good enough”

</div>

## Numbered steps

<div class="checklist-print">

1. **Power and space** — Unplug the dryer or flip the breaker. Give yourself a clear path; do not yank a gas connector or crush a water line on combo units.
2. **Pull carefully** — Slide the dryer straight out a short distance. If the hose binds, stop and loosen clamps rather than stretching a crushed duct.
3. **Lint filter and cabinet** — Empty the trap. Vacuum obvious lint under and behind the machine so it is not pulled back into the intake.
4. **Transition duct type** — Prefer rigid or semi-rigid metal for the short run behind the dryer. Soft foil and vinyl crush easily and trap lint — see [metal vs foil](/ventruncheck/compare/metal-transition-vs-foil-duct).
5. **Length and bends** — Keep the transition as short and straight as the space allows. Extra loops and sharp kinks are common clog starters.
6. **Clamps and joints** — Duct seated on the dryer outlet and on the wall/floor fitting; clamps snug; no large gaps dumping lint under the machine. Avoid screws that poke into the airflow path when you can use proper clamps instead.
7. **Wall or floor fitting** — Look into the house-side opening with a light. Heavy lint mats, bird nesting material, or a near-total plug mean you need more than a wipe of the first foot.
8. **Estimate reach** — If the run goes into a wall, ceiling, or crawl and you cannot see the outdoor end, treat DIY as partial. Long pipes and roof caps usually need a [pro full-run clean](/ventruncheck/guides/diy-vs-pro-dryer-vent-cleaning).
9. **Re-seat and test empty** — Restore a metal transition, push the dryer back without crushing the duct, restore power, and run air-only a few minutes. Check [outdoor flap motion](/ventruncheck/checklist/outdoor-vent-cap-inspection) and smell — no burning odor.
10. **Finish with the pre-use pass** — Before laundry resumes, complete [before you run the dryer again](/ventruncheck/checklist/before-you-run-the-dryer-again). Log date, duct type, and any section you could not reach.

</div>

## Notes

- This checklist is a practical indoor pass, not a code inspection or fire-safety guarantee.
- A clean transition does **not** prove the middle of a long run is clear. Pair it with an outdoor cap check and symptom awareness ([clog signs](/ventruncheck/guides/signs-clogged-dryer-vent)).
- Booster fans do not replace clearing a clog — see [booster fan vs cleaning](/ventruncheck/compare/booster-fan-vs-cleaning-clog).

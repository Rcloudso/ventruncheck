<div class="callout warn">

**Red-flag stop conditions — do not keep running the dryer if any of these are true:**

- Burning smell, smoke, or scorched/brown lint in or behind the dryer
- Cabinet, rear panel, or exhaust air much hotter than your normal loads
- Transition duct melted, crushed flat, disconnected, or still soft foil/vinyl dumping lint
- Outdoor flap stuck shut or packed solid while you keep adding heat cycles
- Gas dryer: gas odor after you move the machine (leave the area and follow your gas utility’s emergency guidance)
- Long or roof-terminated run you cannot reach — unfinished DIY is not “good enough”

</div>

## Numbered steps

<div class="checklist-print">

1. **Stop stacking heat cycles** — If a normal load needs two or three full cycles, pause laundry. Extra heat on a restricted vent is how lint cooks.
2. **Lint screen first** — Empty the trap every load; wipe the recess so the filter seats flat. A packed screen alone can double dry time — see [lint screen vs full vent](/ventruncheck/compare/lint-screen-vs-full-vent-cleaning).
3. **Load size and fabric** — One oversized wet load or heavy towels can look like a “bad dryer.” Retest with a small mixed load before you buy parts.
4. **Room and intake** — Closet installs need makeup air. If the door seals tight while the dryer runs, open it for a test load and note whether time improves.
5. **Behind-the-dryer pass** — Unplug (or kill the breaker). Pull the machine carefully and inspect the short transition: metal preferred, not crushed foil/vinyl; clamps seated; no big lint piles under the unit — use [behind-the-dryer inspection](/ventruncheck/checklist/behind-the-dryer-inspection).
6. **Wall opening glance** — With a light, look into the house-side fitting. Heavy lint mats in the first foot mean the rest of the run is suspect, not “probably fine.”
7. **Outdoor termination** — Check the cap/flap while someone runs air-only a minute: flap should move; screen (if any) not packed; no nest material — [outdoor vent cap checklist](/ventruncheck/checklist/outdoor-vent-cap-inspection).
8. **Symptom cross-check** — Hot laundry room, weak outdoor airflow, and lint at the cap together point to a clog, not a dying heating element — see [signs of a clog](/ventruncheck/guides/signs-clogged-dryer-vent) and [check the vent before replacing the dryer](/ventruncheck/guides/dont-replace-dryer-check-vent-first).
9. **Length and access decision** — Short, visible metal runs: brush/vacuum toward the outdoors if you can finish the whole path. Long runs (~25+ ft equivalent), many elbows, multi-story, or roof caps: hire a **pro full-run clean** with written scope to the termination ([DIY vs pro](/ventruncheck/guides/diy-vs-pro-dryer-vent-cleaning)). Do not keep heat-testing a mid-run plug.
10. **Before laundry resumes** — After any clean or reconnect, complete [before you run the dryer again](/ventruncheck/checklist/before-you-run-the-dryer-again). Log date, what you cleared, and any section you could not reach.

</div>

## Notes

- Slow drying is a decision checklist, not a fire-safety certificate or appliance diagnosis.
- Emptying the lint screen does **not** prove the hose, wall chase, or outdoor hood are clear.
- Booster fans do not fix a clog — see [booster fan vs cleaning](/ventruncheck/compare/booster-fan-vs-cleaning-clog).

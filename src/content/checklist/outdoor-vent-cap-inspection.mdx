<div class="callout warn">

**Red-flag stop conditions — do not keep running the dryer if any of these are true:**

- Burning smell, smoke, or scorched/brown lint at the cap or behind the dryer
- Cap flap welded shut with melted plastic or heavy scorched build-up
- Exhaust air is scorching-hot and weak, or almost no air moves the flap
- You see flames, glowing embers, or dense smoke — leave the area and follow local emergency guidance
- Gas dryer: gas odor after moving or working near the machine (leave, call your gas utility’s emergency line)

</div>

## Numbered steps

<div class="checklist-print">

1. **Access safely** — Only inspect caps you can reach from solid ground or a stable step stool. Skip roof, second-story, or icy/ladder-only terminations — hire someone for those.
2. **Power off if you will touch the cap** — Unplug the dryer or flip the breaker before you pull lint out of the hood. Do not stick tools into a running exhaust.
3. **Watch one empty or short timed cycle** — From outside: does the flap open with a steady push of warm air, flutter weakly, or stay shut? Weak or no motion is a clog signal even if the hood looks “clean.”
4. **Clear the hood face** — Remove lint mats, leaves, and debris from the screen or louvers. Do not oil the flap with cooking spray or petroleum products.
5. **Check the flap hinge** — Flap should swing freely and close when the dryer stops (keeps pests out). Stuck open or stuck shut both need fixing — stuck shut traps heat and lint.
6. **Look for nests and critters** — Birds, rodents, and insects pack the last foot of duct. Soft material, droppings, or feathers mean stop DIY probing deep into the line; call a pro who can clear the full run.
7. **Feel airflow quality** — Steady warm exhaust is the goal. Hot + weak often means a mid-run plug, crushed transition, or too many bends — not only a dirty hood.
8. **Trace back indoors** — If the outdoor cap is clear but clothes still need two cycles, inspect the short metal transition behind the dryer and consider a [full-run clean](/ventruncheck/guides/diy-vs-pro-dryer-vent-cleaning). Foil/vinyl hose issues: [metal vs foil](/ventruncheck/compare/metal-transition-vs-foil-duct).
9. **Log what you found** — Date, flap behavior, lint volume, and whether you only cleaned the hood. Use it for the next [interval](/ventruncheck/guides/how-often-clean-dryer-vent) and any pro quote.
10. **Before laundry resumes** — If you disconnected anything or ran tools into the duct, complete the [before-you-run checklist](/ventruncheck/checklist/before-you-run-the-dryer-again).

</div>

## Notes

- A spotless outdoor cap does **not** prove the middle of a long run is clear. Cap checks are a quick filter, not a full inspection.
- Long runs, roof caps, and hidden chases are easy to under-clean. Prefer a pro for full dryer-to-termination scope when DIY only reaches the ends.
